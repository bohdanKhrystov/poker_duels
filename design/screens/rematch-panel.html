Rematch panel

The product's first surface that sits above another screen (ADR-0123 §8): a standing rematch offer, drawn so it can follow a player onto every screen they hold their room on without ever taking one. Four frames: the offer standing, the accepted span, the room gone, and the screen with the panel set aside — which is the screen, unchanged.

The offer standing

whatever screen the player asked for
Your rival offers a rematch

ADR-0138 §5: the live panel's root is taken out of the document's own flow — position: fixed — so its arrival can never move the screen underneath, and this frame's stand-in stage is what that screen stands for. ADR-0138 §6: the root carries role="status" and nothing about it ever takes focus, sets a tab stop of its own, or listens for a key — a press only reaches it once a player has deliberately moved to one of its two controls. The sentence and both labels are RematchControl's own words, unedited (ADR-0123 §4); "Not now" is copied here rather than imported, because the module it once lived on is gone (ADR-0138 §4). ADR-0143 §1: the line's border and tint are the one accent this card spends — they say what the line is, a standing offer, exactly as the client's own control already colors it, and mark nothing about how carefully to read it.

The accepted span

Rematch. The button changes sides —
dealing hand 1…

The wire sends no frame that says both seats have agreed, so between the press and the new duel the panel shows the same sentence the result screen already shows in that span, in place of the button that just sent OfferRematch. The sentence is split by one <br>, exactly as the merged control splits it, into the two words this card and the client share as separate units — one joined line would be one unit and the two would match nothing. "Not now" still stands: it asserts nothing about the game, so it remains the only way off a panel that never leaves by itself (ADR-0138 §4). No line here is timed and none of this card holds a clock — the three standing states stay exactly where they are until a player or a fresh frame moves them.

The room is gone

whatever screen the player asked for

That duel room is gone.

A press refused because the room has been reaped — ADR-0044 §6's frame that ends a rematch — leaves the panel standing with this sentence in place of the button, plain and unmarked, exactly as the other two states are. "Not now" still stands, for the same reason it stands in the frame before this one: it is the only way off a panel that answers to nothing but a player's own press.

Set aside — the screen, unchanged

the same screen, with nothing taken and nothing added

ADR-0123 §7: a dismissal hides the surface, never the offer — the offer still stands in the room, and a player can still answer it from the room's own screen. What "Not now" buys is exactly this frame: the screen the player was already on, with no panel drawn over it at all. This card holds no motion of any kind on any of its four frames — nothing here fades, slides, pulses or repeats on a cycle — so ADR-0123 §8's requirement that each of the first three sit still under a reduced-motion preference is met by there being nothing to still, not by a rule that quietly does nothing.
